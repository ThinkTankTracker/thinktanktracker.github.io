    Contrary to the traditional argument that primarily relies on the concept of diminishing returns on incremental capital investment ... I offer a different view, grounded in several core concepts: 1) Rising household incomes increase the envelope of economically viable HSR projects, driving improvements in return potential on projects where returns may not have previously been adequate. 2) HSR infrastructure itself contributes to rising societal productivity (and concomitant household incomes) by lowering the cost of travel and enabling broad-based economic activity like tourism and commerce. 3) Wright's Law (learning curves driven by scale) suppresses real construction costs relative to household incomes. This further improves the risk-return profile of incremental rail projects. e.g. learning how to efficiently bore tunnels through mountains from the 2009-2014 GuiGuang project improves the risk/return profile for future projects in mountainous areas like the Xi'an-Ankang line through the Qinling mountains. In essence, the diminishing returns argument makes a critical assumptions mistake — that economies are largely static and project assumptions don't change. But economies are not static, they are dynamic. And incremental project returns analysis must factor in changing variables over time (driven by productivity improvements). This is especially true for long-lived capital projects like HSR, where compound growth in household income can drive huge economic returns in the outer years.post ↗原帖 ↗
