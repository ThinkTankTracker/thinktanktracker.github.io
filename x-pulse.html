    Many people are wildly misinterpreting the results because they don't understand how the NYC public HS admissions process works. Unlike most of America — where the suburban public HS option is based on your school district — in NYC 8th graders apply to public HSes citywide through a system based on rank order by preference. Beyond the three specialized HSes studied in the paper (Stuyvesant, Bronx Science, Brooklyn Tech), there are a handful of other specialized HSes. Then there is a separate application track of non-specialized HSes with many using their own screened process. There is also a third track for art/design schools like La Guardia. This nuance is critical because the study's key methodology is comparing certain metrics for those who just made the cutoff scores and attended the specialized high schools vs. those who just missed the cutoff scores and attended other schools. And what the study finds is that these three specialized HSes did not have a statistically significant effect on those metrics. Most people are then incorrectly interpreting it to conclude that the specialized HSes do not improve outcomes (implicitly over "normie" / mediocre public schools) and "it is all selection effects". While I also have specific issues with how they are defining "outcomes", the part I wanted to address here is the latter: The students that just missed the cutoff scores for the three specialized still tend to end up going to a really competitive set of screened citywide high schools like Bard, Beacon, Millennium, Townsend Harris. So the counterfactual isn’t ending up at a mediocre “regular public school” — a scenario that you can run into for those who live in a mediocre suburban school district. The ones who missed the cutoff (and did not choose private school) typically end up at other highly competitive, high-quality NYC public schools. If anything the point that one should draw from this study (at least with respect to NYC; I am not familiar with Boston) is that the tier of competitive NYC public schools after the three specialized HSes studied in the research are also really great schools that also prepare/develop their students really well. And if you go onto Internet forums like r/SHSAT you will see that it is not uncommon for students to choose competitive non-specialized HSes over specialized ones (e.g. a bright student living in Queens choosing Townsend Harris over a longer commute to attend Brooklyn Tech or Bronx Science).post ↗原帖 ↗
