    @ShortCircuit_KJAdam Tooze makes a useful point about bond markets that often gets inverted. When Germany barely issued new debt (debt brake + surpluses), Bunds became scarce. Bond traders and portfolio managers still need high-quality paper to buy, sell, and hold. So French OATs stepped in as the closest substitute. Demand for French bonds rose, prices went up, yields fell — and France temporarily joined the “high-quality” club alongside Germany. Scarcity of the safest asset doesn’t mean managers love thin markets. It means capital gets pushed into the next-best alternative until that scarcity eases. Once German issuance picked up and perceptions shifted, France’s spreads widened again.
