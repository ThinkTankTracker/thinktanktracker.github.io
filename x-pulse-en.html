    There's a bitter irony in what I'm watching this week. Ukraine — the cause that was supposed to unite Europe — is now being used to divide it. The argument doing the rounds: that Spain's supposedly meagre support for Kyiv explains, even excuses, why other Europeans won't stand with Madrid over Ceuta. Two things need saying. First, the premise is false. Spain has committed close to €17bn in total support to Ukraine — €3.8–4bn of it military — making it the eighth-largest donor in NATO and the fifth in the EU. A fresh €1bn for 2026, co-production of drones and radars with Ukrainian firms, €570m for reconstruction. You can argue Spain should do more relative to its size. That's a fair debate. "Spain has done nothing" is not a debate; it's a falsehood. Second, and more important: even if it were true, so what? And a Europe that only helps those with a clean ledger is not a Union. It's an accountancy firm with a flag. You do not answer an attack on one member's territorial integrity by itemising its invoices. You answer it by showing up. That is the whole idea. Or it was.post ↗
