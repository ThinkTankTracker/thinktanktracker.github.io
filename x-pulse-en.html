    My latest #opEd for the @FT is out. Huge thanks to @TonyTassell for editing it so marvelously. Most people still get renminbi internationalisation wrong. They treat it as one long climb to reserve-currency status. It isn’t. There are three separate mountains: Trade settlement, Borrowing, and actual investment demand China has climbed the first two. The third has barely started — and it’s the only one that matters for real power. You can induce people to invoice in your currency. You can lend them the money, but you cannot force them to want to hold it when times get rough. That’s why foreign ownership of onshore bonds remains under 2%, and central bank reserves in renminbi remain stuck at roughly the same level. Liquidity is thin, hedging is limited, yields are among the lowest in the world, and exit is never fully free. The measures unveiled in Shanghai in June at Luijiazu are the first serious attempt to build the missing plumbing, which this article explains. A lot might happen with global relevance, but it is by no means easy, given how low-yielding the RMB is and how locked in it remains. Hong Kong will remain key. https://www.ft.com/content/5fbd8dad-7e3d-418b-91dc-b61af9436ca6post ↗
