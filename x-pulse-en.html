The underlying inflation measured by trimmed mean was flat +3.6% YoY in June, also below their view. Still, trimmed mean has been gradually rising since the RBA hiked. Hence, the important question is whether inflation would peak in Q2-26 as the Reserve Bank expects, which would have important implications for the monetary policy. Fortunately, capacity shortages have been shrinking gradually, alleviating inflationary pressure. In fact, capacity utilization has been on a downward trend falling to 82.0% in June (Chart 2), converging to the long term-average of 81.4%. Furthermore, the labor market has been cooling faster than the RBA’s expectations. The unemployment rate marginally rose from 4.37% in May to 4.43% in June, above the RBA’s forecast of 4.2%. For sure, employment surprised on the upside increasing by +76.3K in June, lifting the participation rate to 67.0%. However, this was led by part-time employment rising by +47.0K. Possibly, Australians were willing to work more, as their purchasing power reduced by sticky inflation. On the other hand, demand has been holding up well, which could worsen capacity shortage. Softening inflation expectations suggests that the headline inflation could peak in Q2-26, which is in line with the Reserve Bank’s view.
